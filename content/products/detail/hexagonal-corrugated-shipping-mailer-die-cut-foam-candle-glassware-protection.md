The hexagonal geometry is not a decorative choice — it's structural. A hexagon distributes impact forces across 6 faces instead of 4, meaning a corner drop onto a conveyor belt or doorstep concrete spreads the shock vector through a 120° internal angle rather than a 90° right-angle corner that concentrates stress. Combined with a precision die-cut EPE (expanded polyethylene) foam insert that cradles the candle jar in a 360° nest, this mailer consistently passes ISTA 3A drop testing from 1.2m — the standard for single-parcel e-commerce shipping.

## The Hexagonal Advantage

Standard rectangular corrugated mailers are vulnerable at the corners — the most common impact point in automated sorting facilities. A hexagon has no acute corners. Every edge meets at 120°, distributing impact energy across a wider surface area and reducing the peak pressure transmitted to the product inside. In Bincai's in-house drop testing laboratory, hexagonal mailers outperformed equivalent-grade rectangular mailers by 40% in corner-drop survival rate at identical drop heights.

The double-wall B/C flute construction provides two layers of fluting — B-flute (≈3mm) for flat crush resistance and C-flute (≈4mm) for cushioning — bonded with moisture-resistant starch adhesive. Board grade is 200# test minimum (ECT 44), with upgrade paths to 275# and 350# for heavier candle jars or multi-jar sets.

## Quality Control — ISTA 3A Validated

Every production batch goes through our 6-stage QC protocol:

1. **Board Grade Verification**: ECT and burst strength measured against spec, every pallet
2. **Die-Cut Tolerance**: ±1mm on locking tab profiles using calibrated gauges
3. **Glue/Stitch Bond**: Tensile pull test on manufacturer's joint
4. **Foam Insert Fit Check**: Jar insertion/removal force within spec, zero-contact between jar and corrugated walls
5. **ISTA 3A Drop Test**: Random sample — 10-drop sequence from 1.2m, zero breakage accepted
6. **Final AQL 1.5 Visual**: Print registration, crush damage, general appearance
