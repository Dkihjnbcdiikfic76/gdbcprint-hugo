## FAQ

**Q: What does "stamp logo" mean — is it a real letterpress impression or just printed to look like one?**

A: Our stamp logo process uses an authentic Heidelberg Windmill platen letterpress — a genuine relief-printing technique where a magnesium or copper die physically presses into the kraft board at adjustable pressure, creating a tactile deboss (indentation) of 0.15–0.35mm depth simultaneously filled with soy-based ink. This is not a simulated flat-print effect; it's the same traditional letterpress method used by fine stationery printers, producing an impression you can feel with your fingertips on both the front and (if desired) the reverse side of the board. We can also produce blind deboss (no ink, impression only) or foil-stamped logos using the same platen press with heated foil dies for metallic results. Our prepress team will consult with you on artwork preparation — line-art vector files work best for stamp logos, and we recommend minimum 1pt line weight and 6pt positive type for optimal reproduction on textured kraft surfaces.

**Q: How does FSC certification benefit my brand, and can I display the FSC logo on the box?**

A: FSC Chain-of-Custody certification provides independent third-party verification that the paperboard in your packaging traces back to responsibly managed forests — not illegal logging, not deforestation, not conflict timber. When you order from Bincai, we issue a transaction certificate for each production batch that documents the certified fiber content, which you can use in your own sustainability reporting and marketing claims. We can print the FSC Recycled 100% or FSC Mix label directly on your box (subject to FSC trademark approval of your artwork), which consumers increasingly recognize — global consumer surveys show FSC is the most trusted environmental label in packaging. Additionally, FSC certification contributes points toward LEED, BREEAM, and various retailer sustainability scorecards including the Walmart Sustainability Index and Amazon Climate Pledge Friendly program.

**Q: What is the difference between FSC Recycled 100% and FSC Mix kraft board, and which should I choose?**

A: FSC Recycled 100% board is manufactured entirely from post-consumer recovered fiber — paper that consumers have already used and recycled — with zero virgin tree fiber content, making it the strongest circular-economy statement for your packaging. It has a slightly warmer brown tone and marginally higher surface texture compared to FSC Mix board. FSC Mix board contains a blend of FSC-certified virgin fiber, recycled content, and/or controlled wood, offering a slightly smoother, more consistent printing surface while still guaranteeing responsible sourcing. Both options carry FSC certification and can bear the respective FSC label on your box. We recommend FSC Recycled 100% for brands whose core value proposition is maximum environmental responsibility, and FSC Mix when you need the smoothest possible surface for fine-detail CMYK printing while maintaining credible sustainability credentials. Either way, our sales team can supply physical swatch samples so you can compare the look and feel side by side.

**Q: Can the entire box be recycled in standard curbside paper streams, including after printing with soy inks?**

A: Yes — our FSC-certified kraft boxes printed with soy-based inks and finished without plastic lamination are fully compatible with standard curbside paper and cardboard recycling streams worldwide. Soy-based and water-based inks de-ink effectively during the repulping process at paper recycling mills, and the natural kraft fibers maintain sufficient strength for 4–7 recycling cycles. We specifically avoid PE lamination, UV-cured coatings, and synthetic adhesives on our eco-box range to preserve end-of-life recyclability. For maximum recycling compatibility, we recommend selecting the uncoated natural finish or aqueous (water-based) coating options, both of which are repulpable. If your customers are in regions with industrial composting programs, the boxes are also suitable for organic waste collection as the board and soy inks will biodegrade — though we recommend removing any separate adhesive labels or stickers before composting.
